Sailing

A multihull regatta from the air — rainbow sails, lifted hulls, and a horizon that will not stay level.

Role
Drone operator, camera, edit
Event
Double Trouble Multihull Regatta
Location
Whitianga, New Zealand
Year
2025

The idea

Sailing is almost always filmed from the shore, where it looks slow. Flown alongside instead, a Hobie catamaran at speed is the opposite of slow: the windward hull lifts clear, the wake tears open behind it, and the horizon rolls because the camera is rolling with it.

This is two days of the Double Trouble Multihull Regatta at Whitianga. A fleet of Hobies in competition is a gift to a camera — the sails are the brightest objects for miles, the boats are close enough together to give each other scale, and nobody is sailing conservatively.

Flying it

  • Fly alongside and slightly above, matching the boat's speed, rather than orbiting it — an orbit reads as a drone shot; a chase reads as a point of view.
  • Let the horizon tilt. Levelling every frame in post would remove the only cue that the boat is heeling hard.
  • Shoot into the bay so the land gives the water a scale; open sea alone flattens out.
  • Keep the aircraft upwind of the spray. Salt ends a day faster than a flat battery.
The chase

The horizon never settles, because the camera is flying the same water the boat is sailing.

Colour does the composition

Green water, grey sky, dark headland: on their own these are a muted, almost monochrome palette. The sails are the only saturated thing in the frame, so most of the framing decisions came down to where the sail sits against that flat background and how much of the water it is allowed to own.

The overheads

The frames shot from directly above a single boat are the closest this project gets to the still work elsewhere in the portfolio. From straight down there is no horizon, no sense of speed and no weather — just a coloured triangle, a white wake and a field of green. They stop being footage and start being composition, which is why they are in here as photographs rather than as a sequence in the cut.

Before the drone

Two years before the aerial coverage, the same fleet was photographed from the water and the shore with a long lens. It is a completely different way to see the boats: no overheads, no graphic flat shapes, just weather, distance, and crews working hard at the edge of the frame.
